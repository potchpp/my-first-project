## Company snapshot
Largest US credit card issuer by card loan balance; it closed the Discover purchase on May 18, 2025 and now owns the Discover network, PULSE and Diners Club (10-K:20-26). Credit Card is 74% of FY2025 segment revenue ($39,560M of $53,434M total; 10-K:1508, 720). The reason to follow it: it is the only large US issuer that is moving its own cards onto a network it owns.

## Fundamentals signal
- **Revenue durability:** revenue $53,434M in FY2025 vs $39,112M in FY2024 (10-K:720), the jump mostly Discover. Card revenue is a repeat-balance book; no retention data in the sources.
- **Margin trend:** NIM 7.84% / 6.88% / 6.63% for FY2025 / 2024 / 2023 (10-K:824, 1118); 8.01% in Q2 2026 (Q2-26 ER). Efficiency ratio 57.08% vs 54.93% (10-K:842), worse as integration costs ($1.1B vs $234M, 10-K:1301) and intangibles amortization ($1,326M vs $77M, 10-K:1278) hit.
- **Capital allocation:** FY2025 dividends ~$1.5B and buybacks ~$3.8B (10-K:962, derived); new $16B buyback authorization effective Oct 21, 2025 (10-K:962). CET1 14.3% at FY2025 (10-K:899) vs 9.0% minimum incl. buffer (10-K:174). Brex deal $5.15B (cash plus ~10.6M shares, 10-K:51-53).
- **Returns:** ROTCE 3.16% in FY2025 vs 11.18% in 2024 and 13.04% in 2023 (10-K:836). Why: average common equity $89,286M vs $54,953M (+62%), an $8.8B day-one allowance on acquired loans (10-K:1248), integration and amortization. The total net charge-off rate went the other way: 3.30% vs 3.39% (10-K:851). So the FY2025 drop was deal accounting, not credit decay.

## Network vs Visa/Mastercard (counter-positioning test)
- **Moved so far:** debit reissuance onto the Discover network "substantially completed" (10-K:41); credit: new cards on the network by mid-2026, existing cards early 2027, "subject to testing" (Call:64-66, 288-290; stale, status in 2026 unconfirmed). Global Payment Network volume $401,775M in FY2025, a partial year (10-K:1821); about $175B in Q4 2025 (Call:132). No % of COF volume migrated is in the sources.
- **Economics:** on Bank-issued cards on the network, part of the merchant fee passes through to the bank (10-K:93); net discount and interchange $6,443M vs $4,882M (10-K:1219). Synergy targets: $2.7B pre-tax by 2027, $1.5B expense plus $1.2B network *(web, Banking Dive, 2024; later update not found)*; the later framing is $2.5B net, remainder by 2H 2027 *(web, MarketBeat 2026-09-17)*.
- **7 Powers verdict, Counter-positioning: real but narrow.** For: Visa and Mastercard are neutral multi-issuer networks, so copying an issuer-owned network would cost them issuers that fund their incentives. Against: Amex has run a closed loop for decades; they can answer with price and incentives on the credit volume still to move; the wider network lacks acceptance breadth (Network economies, a power Visa and Mastercard hold; US acceptance ~99% is a company claim, weaker abroad; unverified, *web, CardRates 2026*). The defensible piece is the issuer-owned slice, which is mostly COF's own volume.
- **Share:** US purchase volume across four networks $11.463T in FY2025 (Nilson, valuation agent); COF purchase volume $828B (10-K:816, 1594), about 7%. The price implies ~9-10% by 2027, mostly from migrating its own volume (valuation agent, inference).
- **Merchant antitrust class action** filed 2026-09-30 (EDNY) names COF alongside Visa, Mastercard and four large banks; allegations unproven *(web, Courthouse News, 2026-10)*.

## Addressable market

## Bull / Bear
**Bull**
- FY2025 earnings fell on deal accounting, not credit: ROTCE 3.16% (10-K:836) with an $8.8B day-one allowance, $1.1B integration, intangibles amortization $1,326M (10-K:1248, 1301, 1278), while the NCO rate fell 9 bps (10-K:851). Q2 2026 GAAP EPS $4.73 and adjusted efficiency 51.38% vs 57.05% GAAP show the drag is rolling off (Q2-26 ER).
- Capital gives a floor: CET1 13.7% vs 9.0% minimum (Q2-26 ER; 10-K:174), buybacks $5.2B in H1 2026 *(web, SEC 10-Q for the quarter ended 2026-06-30)*, $16B authorized (10-K:962).
- The issuer-owned network is a counter-positioning that Visa and Mastercard cannot copy without hurting their own issuer base (valuation agent).

**Bear**
- Credit is most of the stock: 1 pt of charge-offs is about $4.6B pre-tax, ~$5.6/share, ~25% of EPS (valuation agent, inference); the price tolerates ~3.4-3.6% before ROTCE breaks vs Q2 at ~3.2% (valuation agent, inference), a cushion of 0.2-0.4 pt.
- Q2 is flattered: a $662M reserve release (~$0.8/share, inference), so adjusted EPS $5.81 is nearer $5.0 without it; ROTCE has one quarter of history, and the ~18-19% run-rate (18% GAAP, ~19% ex reserve release) is the valuation agent's own arithmetic, not a reported figure.
- The network is a rounding item (about $15/share of ~$200, and all Discover synergies together about $33; valuation agent), and Visa and Mastercard hold the acceptance breadth. Regulation is open: a 10% card-rate cap call in January 2026 cut the stock ~6.4% in a day *(web, CNBC 2026-01-12)*; CFPB late-fee review *(web, Law360 2026-07-07)*.

## Valuation
- **Lens:** a lender is valued on price to tangible book vs ROTCE, plus P/E on normalized earnings. Price $200.67 on 2026-10-16 *(web, Yahoo Finance; prior close $198.86)*; shares 625.1M at 2025 year-end (10-K:773), so market cap ~$125B (inference, before 2026 buybacks). TBVPS $107.72 at 2025 year-end (10-K:780) → 1.86x; ~1.9x on a Q2 2026 TBVPS of $105.21 *(web, Barchart earnings preview, 2026-10; unconfirmed)*. Normalized EPS ~$5.0/quarter, ~$20/year → ~10x (inference).
- **Implied expectation:** with cost of equity 11% and growth 3-4% (both assumptions), the price needs ROTCE of ~17-18%. The Q2 2026 run-rate is ~18-19% (valuation agent, EPS over TBVPS, inference). The price needs the run-rate to hold, not rise.
- **Verdict: Deserved.** The price asks for continuation, and the part that is new (network synergies) is only a small slice of it.
- **Falsifying number:** Q4 2026 adjusted diluted EPS **below $4.75** (≈$19 annualized ≈ 17.6% ROTCE, under the 17-18% bar), reported late January 2027. Current: $5.81 for Q2 2026 (≈$5.0 without the reserve release). Note: this rests on the valuation agent's inferred ROTCE bar, not on a reported figure.

**How much of the thesis is network vs lending (stated plainly):** ~16% of implied value (~$33/share) is Discover synergies, of which network ~$15 (7%) and expense ~$18 (9%); ~84% (~$167/share) is the lending book and cycle (valuation agent, inference). The investor avoids macro-dependent theses: this one is mostly that. The network is the company-specific part, and it is small.

## Kill conditions
1. Domestic card net charge-off rate above 5.0% for two consecutive monthly 8-K reports, with 30+ day delinquency above 4.0% (latest: 4.16% / 3.57% for August 2026, *web*). `[bounded ~25% of EPS per point; open-ended if it persists]`. Bull cannot call this seasonal noise once January's 5.04% *(web)* is already in the base.
2. A card interest-rate cap becoming law, or a CFPB late-fee rule that cuts card revenue materially. `[open-ended]`
3. Credit-card migration to the Discover network not live by end of 2027, or network synergy below $0.6B annualized by then. `[bounded ~7%]` (network value share, valuation agent). Expense synergies could still deliver, so this alone is a Bear item unless combined with 1.

## What to ask
1. What share of COF debit and credit volume sits on the Discover network today, and what net take does it earn — is the $1.2B network synergy visible in the numbers yet?
2. Strip the reserve release and integration items: what is a through-the-cycle ROTCE at a ~4.5% card charge-off rate, and is it above the ~17-18% bar?
3. What happens to the debit synergy if debit fee rules tighten, and does Brex dilute capital faster than the buyback shrinks it?
