## Company snapshot
Visa runs a four-party card network: it is not an issuer, extends no credit and bears no credit risk (10-K:76). Revenue comes from service, data processing, international transaction and other (value-added services, VAS) fees, less client incentives (10-K:926-939). FY2025 net revenue was $40,000M, +11% (10-K:568). It matters because it sits on a large share of global consumer and commercial payment flows, so growth in spend, cross-border and new services flows through it.

## Fundamentals signal
- **Revenue durability.** FY2025/24/23 net revenue $40,000M / $35,926M / $32,653M (10-K:926-939). Data processing (largest line) $19,993M, +13%; service $17,539M, +9%; international transaction $14,166M, +12%; other $4,053M, +27% (10-K:926-939). VAS $10.9B, +24% (10-K:953), about 27% of FY2025 net revenue (derived). Processed transactions 257,545M, +10% (10-K:861). Fees are charged on volume and transaction counts through the revenue lines defined at 10-K:52-70, so revenue repeats rather than being won anew each year.
- **Margin trend.** Non-GAAP net revenue less opex 67.7% / 67.7% / 67.9% for FY25/24/23 (derived from 10-K:575-593, :675). GAAP fell to 60.0% from 65.7%, because the litigation provision was $2,562M vs $462M (10-K:1008). The GAAP dip is litigation, not operations; the non-GAAP reconciliation excludes $2,533M against $2,562M on the P&L line, a $29M gap unexplained in the excerpt (10-K:673 vs :1008). Non-GAAP EPS $11.47, +14% (10-K:583); GAAP EPS $10.20, +5% (10-K:574).
- **Client incentives** grew +14% vs net revenue +11% (10-K:937); 28.3% of gross revenue vs 27.4% in FY23 (derived).
- **Capital allocation.** Operating cash flow $23,059M (10-K:1113). Buybacks $18.2B for 54M shares (~$337/share, derived); $30.0B authorised Apr 2025 (10-K:609). Dividends $4.6B (10-K:1153). Buybacks plus dividends ($22.8B, derived) are about 99% of operating cash flow. Senior notes $25.4B vs cash $17.2B (10-K:1127, :1157). Capex and FCF are not in the excerpt, so no FCF yield is given.
- **Scale and network effects (7 Powers), shown not asserted.** Visa processed 258B of 329B total brand transactions; the 329B averages ~901M/day (10-K:16). Margin held in a 0.2 pt band while net revenue rose 22% (derived from the figures above). That is scale economies as stability, not widening: FQ2 opex +17% matched revenue +17% (Q2:10, :38), no operating leverage that quarter.
- **Cross-border share of revenue.** The 10-K does not state a cross-border % of net revenue (10-K:519 calls it "a significant part"). Best proxy: international transaction revenue is 35.4% of FY2025 net revenue ($14,166M / $40,000M; 35.3% FY24, 35.6% FY23; derived). It is an accounting line, not the same as cross-border volume or the geographic split (International = 60.9% of net revenue, derived from 10-K:890-894).

## Bull / Bear
**Bull** (bull_researcher)
- Scale shown in numbers: 258B of 329B brand transactions processed by Visa (10-K:16), and non-GAAP margin flat at ~67.7% through +22% revenue (derived). The only bear reply to that is "flat, not rising", which concedes durability. This is the bull point with no bear counter.
- Rails threat is small today: stablecoin settlement of $7B annualised is ~0.04% of $17T volume (Q2:130; 10-K:18; derived). The basis of the $17T figure is not reconciled to the table (10-K:779 gives $16,383B total nominal volume TTM Jun-2025). Entrants route through Visa: settlement went from $2.5B+ (10-K:248) to $7B (Q2:130), and 160+ stablecoin-linked card programs run on Visa (Q2:32). A 5% volume diversion would cost ~5% of revenue, roughly 1 pt a year over 5 years (inferred).
- Litigation lands mostly on issuers, not Visa's revenue line (10-K:76, :365). Unresolved covered-claim interchange fell to $39.4B from $49.6B (10-K:607). US is ~39% of net revenue (derived, 10-K:890-894); the 60.9% international part is untouched by US rules.

**Bear** (bear_researcher)
- Cross-border is the one lever with no hedge in the file. International transaction revenue is 35.4% of net revenue (derived); travel slowed to +5% quarter-to-date (Q2:166). FQ3 cross-border +12% but international transaction revenue +6% *(web)* suggests revenue is not converting at the same rate. If it stalled, net revenue growth would lose about 3.9 pts (35.4% x 11%, derived, all else equal).
- Incentives outgrow revenue: +14% vs +11% in FY2025 (10-K:937), and +18% vs +14% in FQ3 *(web)*, while 2H pricing changes are needed to offset (Q2:172, :180). Litigation recurs: provisions $2,562M vs $462M (10-K:1008), plus $237M in FQ3 *(web)*. DOJ debit case ongoing (Bloomberg Law, 2026-06-30 *(web)*) and not covered in the 10-K excerpt.
- Cash return is near 100% of operating cash flow, with $25.4B notes outstanding (10-K:1113, :1157). Buybacks supply ~3 pts of the ~12% EPS growth the price implies (inferred), so about a quarter of it is bought rather than earned. Rails substitution cannot be sized: no stablecoin or A2A profit is disclosed (Q2:214-220), and the 10-K itself says stablecoins "could potentially disrupt" cross-border and B2B (10-K:479).

## Valuation
- **Lens:** earnings multiple vs the EPS growth the price implies (a mature, profitable network; buybacks sit inside EPS). Non-GAAP EPS is used because GAAP is depressed by litigation (10-K:574, :583).
- **Price:** ~$360.66 on 2026-10-02 *(web, search snippet, no named publisher; (unverified))*; $367.39 on 2026-09-10 (InvestSmart *(web)*). No quote for 16–17 Oct found. Market cap $653.65B at $364.40 on 2026-07-16 (MarketBeat *(web)*, share basis not stated); no share count in the local sources, so no market cap is computed here.
- **Multiples (derived from ~$360):** 360 / $11.47 FY2025 non-GAAP EPS (10-K:583) = 31.4x. FY2026E EPS ≈ $11.47 x 1.13 = $12.96 (my assumption: low teens per guidance) = 27.8x. Third-party 10-year forward P/E average ~33.6x (A.L. Capital, 2026-06-19 *(web)*).
- **Implied expectation:** to earn 8.5%/yr for 5 years with the multiple falling to 24x, price reaches $541 and EPS must reach $22.5, i.e. ~12% a year (about 8% a year at a 28x exit). About 3 pts of that can come from buybacks (inferred). That is about 9–10% a year net revenue growth with stable margins, ~$72B by FY2031 (inferred). Required growth is below FY2025's 14% EPS growth (10-K:583) and FQ2's 20% (Q2:12), and in line with FY2026 guidance. This is continuation, not acceleration; the risk sits in the multiple falling faster than assumed.
- **Verdict: Deserved.** Consensus agrees: 38 buy / 3 hold / 0 sell, average target $413.45 (Walnut, Aug 2026 *(web)*); that is a sentiment fact, not evidence for the verdict.
- **Falsifying number:** FY2026 non-GAAP diluted EPS growth **below 10%**, which is **below $12.62** against $11.47 (10-K:583; 11.47 x 1.10, derived). Deadline: the FQ4/FY2026 release, ~2026-10-27 *(web: therighttrader.com earnings calendar, read 2026-10-17; company has not confirmed in local sources)*. Current value: not yet reported; guidance is low teens on an adjusted basis (Q2:168); FQ2 +20% (Q2:12), FQ3 +11% *(web)*. A second bar: FQ4 net revenue growth below 9% (FQ2: 17%, Q2:10). Falsifier direction follows the rule: "Deserved" is disproved by a number below the bar.

## Addressable market
- **TAM:** Visa sizes consumer spend at more than $40T (ex-Russia/China), of which more than $20T is underserved (10-K:88), and ~$200T of total payment flows (10-K:162). The $200T rests on third-party studies as of 2022 (10-K:172): older than 2 years, treat as stale.
- **Bottom-up check:** FY2025 volume $17T (10-K:18; basis unreconciled, includes cash) / $200T = 8.5% (derived). Revenue yield $40.0B / $17T = 0.235% (derived). $72B of FY2031 revenue at that yield needs ~$30.6T of volume.
- **Share vs implied share:** current ~8.5%; implied ~12.6% if TAM grows 4%/yr to ~$243T (my assumption). Alternative path: volume +9%/yr (Q2:14) for the 6 years FY2025 to FY2031 gives ~$28.5T (17 x 1.09^6), so yield must rise to ~0.253% (72 / 28.5T), which leans on VAS (+27% cc, Q2:26). Implied share rises from today's, but no figure here shows a peer share ceiling.
- **Contrarian estimate:** no sourced external bear estimate found. Nearest is Visa's own risk language on account-to-account (A2A) and real-time rails: RTP networks in 80+ countries, FedNow, PIX, UPI, "potential disintermediation" (10-K:349, :425, :477-479). Treat as qualitative.

## Variant perception
- *Thesis metric:* non-GAAP EPS growth (the falsifier above), driven by payments volume and cross-border growth, with client incentives as a share of gross revenue (28.3% in FY2025, derived) as the cross-check.
- *Where we differ from consensus:* nothing. We hold the consensus view; there is no expectation gap to earn here. What is ours is the lens: V is a toll road whose cross-border line (35.4% of net revenue, proxy) is the sensitivity, and rails substitution is the long-dated risk that cannot yet be sized.

## Kill conditions
1. **Credit Card Competition Act enacted, or a DOJ debit-case remedy that forces multi-network routing on credit.** `[open-ended]` This is the primary one: the strongest bear point with no sizing in the file (no remedy or revenue sizing exists; Reg II shows a rule change can reset economics, 10-K:369, :375). Status *(web)*: CCCA reintroduced 2026-01-13 and endorsed by the President (Durbin office); an attempt to attach it to a housing bill failed in March 2026 (Payments Dive); DOJ case ongoing.
2. **Non-Visa rails (stablecoin, A2A, real-time) take material cross-border or B2B volume with Visa's net revenue yield falling.** `[open-ended]` Trigger as a number: yield below ~0.20% (0.235% in FY2025, derived; 10-K:18 basis unreconciled) for a full fiscal year, or Visa reporting disclosed stablecoin settlement above ~1% of payments volume with revenue per dollar falling. Today: $7B run rate ≈ 0.04% (Q2:130). See [[CRCL]] for the issuer side of the same rails.
3. **Net revenue growth below 5% for a full fiscal year with no one-off cause** (cross-border stall plus incentives plus pricing failure). `[bounded ~40%]` — rough size: a multiple that compresses toward ~20x on ~5% growth (inferred); close to the bar for a kill, so it is tracked here but also lives in Bear.
Cross-border slowing, incentives rising and MDL damages (~$6.24B, amended settlement preliminarily approved 2026-06-09, Visa statement *(web)*, about 27% of one year of operating cash flow, derived) are bounded Bear points, not kills.

## What to ask
1. If cross-border e-commerce (+13% in FQ2, Q2:22; +16% in FQ3 *(web)*, Yahoo Finance ~2026-07-28) offsets slower travel but international transaction revenue grows only +6%, which part of the yield is falling: FX, mix, or incentives, and is that rate stable over 3 years?
2. Can Visa disclose profit per dollar on stablecoin settlement and A2A, not just run rate? Without it, kill condition 2 cannot be tracked until it is too late.
3. After the MDL amended settlement (~10bp cut in average effective credit interchange for 5 years, per Visa's MDL statement, corporate.visa.com, read 2026-10-17 *(web)*), what is left for the CCCA and DOJ cases to take, and does the settlement lower the odds of either?
